# The content engine: review of the strategy, and a plan to build it

**Status:** plan only. Nothing here has been built.
**Written:** 10 November 2026, against `master` at `4b70c7f` and a read-only look at
`xhybrid-dev/hybridx-app` (the app behind app.hybridx.club and the store apps).
**Reviewing:** "HybridX mega strategy" (.docx), called *the proposal* below. It
proposes a daily, AI-written SEO guide, generated by Gemini from Search Console
data fetched through the `gum` CLI, plus three lead-capture calculators, an
internal linker and PostHog conversion tracking.

---

## 1. The short version

**The goal is right.** Searchers who arrive with a specific training problem,
solve it with a tool, and get a plan to fix it are HybridX's best customers. The
site's strongest pages are already of that kind: the ATHX endurance calculator,
the Glasgow pacing preview, VDOT, and the nerdy-bits maths. Choosing topics from
Search Console, writing to a set editorial standard, generating structured output
rather than free text, linking internally and measuring tool use are all sound
ideas.

**As written, the mechanics would do harm:**

1. **A new AI article every day, published without anyone reading it, is the
   pattern Google's scaled content abuse policy describes.** The damage is not
   limited to the new pages. Site-wide quality signals can pull down
   `/free-hyrox-plan`, `/how-to-train-for-hyrox` and the calculators, which are
   where today's search traffic comes from. The site has fewer than 40
   indexable pages, so a year of daily posts would leave about 90% of it
   machine-written.
2. **It was written for a different site:** `hybridx.app`, a static host, MDX,
   Python, PostHog and a teal palette. All of those are wrong here, and most of
   what it asks for already exists here in some form (section 2).
3. **The topic picker would create pages that compete with your existing
   pages.** It never asks which of your URLs already ranks for a query, and 11
   of its 30 seed topics overlap a page you already have or another seed
   (section 9).
4. **The calculator calls to action promise things the app cannot do yet**
   ("load this target into HybridX", "this exact schedule, progressed every
   week"). They also point people at app stores, where the app deliberately
   sells nothing (section 6).

**The plan below keeps the goal and changes the mechanics:**

- **Tools first.** Guides exist to bring people to the tools, and the tools
  convert.
- **AI drafts, you approve.** Two or three guides a week, not seven, and never
  auto-published.
- **Built inside this repo's stack:** Next.js, Firestore, `/admin`, the magnet
  machinery, the hourly maintenance job, the existing analytics and the existing
  Gemini key. No Python, no `gum`, no PostHog.
- **Seven phases, each shippable on its own.** Automated drafting comes last,
  after the guide format and the review flow have been proven by hand.
- **Twelve decisions only you can make** (section 10). The first phase needs
  answers to five of them: 1, 2, 5, 9 and 10.

## 3. Problems and risks, most serious first

### 3.1 Search quality policy (the largest risk)

Google's spam policies define scaled content abuse as producing many pages
mainly to manipulate rankings rather than to help people, and they name
generative AI as one way it happens. The policy does not care how a page was
made; it cares about volume that outruns value. Secondary sources describe spam
updates in June and August 2026, and a May 2026 clarification that the same
policies now govern AI Overviews and AI Mode.

A daily, keyword-led, unreviewed article stream is the textbook case. The risk
is asymmetric: a successful guide adds some traffic, while a site-wide quality
demotion takes away the traffic you already have.

**How it would show up:** in Search Console's Pages report, a growing "Crawled –
currently not indexed" count for the new guides. Then, after a core or spam
update, falling impressions on pages that have nothing to do with the guides.

**Mitigations, all in this plan:**

- Every guide is read, edited and approved by a person.
- Two or three guides a week.
- Every guide carries something a model cannot supply: a working tool, real
  numbers from the facts registry, or your coach's note.
- A real author byline.
- A retire-and-merge path for pages that do not earn their place.
- Stop rules (section 7.4).

### 3.2 Cannibalisation

`harvest.py` only checks for duplicates against `data/sitemap-index.json`, the
guides it wrote itself. A "striking distance" query (position 8 to 25) means one
of *your* pages already ranks for it. The right move is usually to improve that
page, not to publish a second one competing for the same query.

The fix is to ask Search Console for `dimensions: ["query", "page"]`, then route
each query either to a *refresh* of the page that already ranks or to a *new
guide*. The seed list has the same problem; section 9 maps each of the 30 seeds
against what exists.

### 3.3 Accuracy, safety and staleness

- **HYROX standards change by season.** The site already has
  `/hyrox-rule-changes-2026`. The proposal's bible lists only the Men's and
  Women's Open weights. It omits the farmers carry and sandbag loads and every
  Pro, Doubles and Relay figure.
- **The bible contains corrupted text:** "programme, kilometre,门槛/kettlebell".
  That suggests the proposal itself was machine-written and not proofread, which
  is exactly what an unreviewed daily engine would publish.
- **ATHX zone names should come from `src/lib/athx-content.ts`**: Strength,
  Endurance and MetCon X.
- **Some topics need real data, not a model.** "Good HYROX time for beginners by
  age" is one. A model asked for it will make the numbers up.
- **Fitness advice is health-adjacent.** Bad loading advice can injure someone.
  The bible's rules are right in spirit, but "6 to 24 hours" is too wide a range
  to be a rule.

**Mitigation:** facts live in typed registries in code, not in prose, and a
checker compares every number in a draft against them (section 4.3).

### 3.4 Review capacity

The binding constraint is your time, not compute. Reading and fixing a
1,500-word technical guide properly takes 30 to 45 minutes. A daily cadence
means 4 to 5 hours a week, every week, or reviews get skipped, which brings back
risk 3.1. Plan for two or three a week.

### 3.5 Security

- **MDX is code.** A model writing MDX that is compiled at build time can emit
  imports and expressions. The inputs include text anyone can influence: a
  Search Console query is whatever somebody typed into Google, and later inputs
  may include questions from users.
- **Mitigation:** the model returns typed JSON blocks. The site renders them
  with its own components, with no raw HTML, no MDX, and links checked against an
  allowlist. The generator never gets write access to the repo.

### 3.6 `gum`

- **I could not reach gumcli.dev from this session** (it is outside the network
  allowlist). The closest match a search turned up is `github.com/ehmo/gum`, a
  third-party CLI and MCP server for Google APIs (v1.0, licence listed as
  "other"). It is not Google software.
- **Its desktop OAuth flow keeps tokens in the OS keychain.** That works on a
  laptop and not in an unattended server or CI job. The proposal's own daily
  runner could not have used it.
- **Its "Trends daily" call returns Britain's trending searches of the day.**
  That means football and news, with no signal for "sled push without a sled".
- **The name clashes with `charmbracelet/gum`,** a popular shell tool, which
  invites installing the wrong thing.
- **What is actually needed is two API calls:** `searchanalytics.query` and
  `urlInspection.index.inspect`. Both are a few lines with Google's own Node
  client and a service account (section 4.9).
- `gum` is still fine as an optional MCP server on your own laptop for exploring
  Search Console by hand. Nothing in the plan depends on it.

### 3.7 Deploy coupling

On this host, committing generated files to `master` means:

- one production rollout a day, shipping whatever else is on `master` that day;
- a malformed generated file failing the build for the whole site;
- every publish waiting the length of a full build.

Keeping content in Firestore removes all three (section 4.2).

### 3.8 Analytics and privacy

- **PostHog would be the third analytics system**, another data processor to
  name in the privacy policy, and a change to the CSP.
- **"No cookie banner required in cookieless memory mode" is not safe to assume
  in the UK.** PECR covers storing or accessing information on a device, not
  just cookies.
- **The site took a deliberate consent position** (ENTRY_FUNNEL_NOTES, consent
  banner) and is still waiting on its privacy sign-off.

Everything the proposal wants to measure can be measured with what exists
(section 7).

### 3.9 Conflict with the shop

The Glasgow 2027 Pacing Pack (£5) is on sale until 14 March 2027. A free
calculator giving station-by-station targets for any finish time undercuts it.
Section 5.3 sets out the options.

### 3.10 The apps

- **The store apps never sell memberships.** Purchase happens on the website
  (`useIsNativeApp`, `docs/MOBILE_RELEASE.md`). A guide CTA that sends a cold
  visitor to a store loses them at a login screen.
- **There are no universal links or app links,** so links cannot open the app.
- **The app has no target finish time,** and programmes are picked from a fixed
  library rather than built to order. The proposal's CTA copy would over-promise.
- **App signup records no source,** so no trial can be traced back to a guide.
- **The app repo's own notes say Apple may still insist on in-app purchase.**
  If it does, prices and CTAs change.

### 3.11 AI answers absorb informational clicks

"How many wall balls in HYROX" gets answered on the results page. A pacing
table built from *your* finish time does not. This is another reason to put the
effort into tools, with guides as the way in.

### 3.12 Brand and claim consistency

- `/app` says "14-day free trial"; `public/llms.txt` says "first month free".
- `llms.txt` claims "over 2,000 athletes".

A content engine repeats whatever it is given at scale. Fix the source facts
before it starts. Also use "HYROX" and "ATHX" descriptively, never in a way that
implies affiliation (the existing pages already do this correctly).

## 4. The recommended design

### 4.1 Shape

```
 Search Console (query x page) ─┐
 Topic map (every existing page)├─▶ weekly shortlist ─▶ you pick 2–3
 Seed list (fallback)           ┘                          │
                                                           ▼
 Facts registry ──▶ BRIEF ──▶ DRAFT ──▶ CHECKS ──▶ MODEL REVIEW ──▶ YOUR REVIEW ──▶ PUBLISH
 Coaching bible ──┘  (code+AI)  (Gemini) (code)    (2nd model)     (/admin/guides)   │
                                                                                     ▼
                         Firestore guides/{slug} status=published ──▶ /guides/[slug] (cached)
                                                                     sitemap.xml, related links,
                                                                     link suggestions for older pages
                         +3 / +7 / +14 / +30 days: URL inspection, clicks, leads ──▶ /admin/guides
```

Each stage runs as one step of the existing hourly maintenance job and saves its
result before the next begins. A failure stops that guide and nothing else.

### 4.2 Where content lives: Firestore, behind an admin review queue

Recommended over committing files to git:

| | Firestore + `/admin/guides` | Generated files committed to git |
|---|---|---|
| Preview before publishing | Rendered exactly as it will appear, on a draft route behind admin sign-in | None: App Hosting has no per-PR preview environments |
| Review on a phone | A form built for it | GitHub's diff view of prose |
| Effect of a bad draft | That guide fails to render | The build fails and the whole site stops deploying |
| Time to publish | Seconds (cache revalidation) | One full App Hosting build |
| History | A revision subcollection per guide | git history |

**Collections** (all server-only: the client has no access):

- **`guides/{slug}`**:
  - lifecycle: `status` (`draft`, `in_review`, `published` or `retired`) and
    `redirectTo`;
  - content: `blocks` (section 4.6), and `meta` (title, description, primary and
    secondary queries, pillar, calculator, CTA variant);
  - provenance: `factsUsed` (ids and versions), `sources`, `author`,
    `reviewedBy`, `reviewedAt`, `publishedAt`, `updatedAt`, `revision`;
  - history: subcollection `revisions/{n}`.
- **`guideRuns/{id}`:** stage, inputs, model, token counts, check results, error.
- **`guideTopics/{id}`:** harvested candidates, with a score, a kind (`new` or
  `refresh`), the page that currently ranks, and a status (`shortlisted`,
  `chosen`, or `rejected` with a reason, so it is not offered again).
- **`guideMetrics/{slug}__{date}`:**
  - from Search Console: clicks, impressions, position, inspection verdict;
  - from the site: tool uses, CTA clicks, captured leads.

**Rendering.** The guide page reads the published document through the Admin
SDK, cached by tag, and publishing revalidates that tag. Nothing reads Firestore
at build time, so content can never break a build.

**Backups.** Use a scheduled Firestore export, plus an "Export as Markdown"
button in admin for anything you want in git.

**What you give up.** Guide text is not in git, so Claude Code edits guides
through admin or a script rather than by editing files. That is a fair trade for
review and preview.

### 4.3 What the engine reads

**Facts registry: `src/lib/facts/`.** Each fact has an id, a value, a source and
a `checkedAt` date.

- HYROX standards by season and division: sled push and pull loads (including
  the sled), farmers carry, sandbag, wall ball weight and target, and distances.
  Reuse `hyrox-rules-2026.ts` for the rules.
- ATHX zones: reuse `athx-content.ts`.
- Product facts: price, trial length, what the app does, and a "Not claimed"
  list in the style of `una-family.ts`.

The generator is given only the facts relevant to a guide, and the checker
verifies every number against them. When a fact changes, a query lists every
guide that used it, so you know what to re-review.

**Topic map: `src/lib/guides/topics.ts`.** Every existing indexable page, with
its pillar and the queries it targets (seeded from `sitemap.ts`), plus each
published guide. This is how the harvester knows what you already cover.

**Coaching bible: `src/lib/guides/bible.ts`.** Kept as a TypeScript string so
it is bundled. Reading a `.md` file at runtime would need
`outputFileTracingIncludes`, the trap the race card's PDF already fell into.
Section 8 sets out what it should contain.

### 4.4 Topic selection (weekly)

1. Pull 28 days of Search Console data ending three days ago, with
   `dimensions: ["query", "page"]` (up to 25,000 rows per request).
2. Normalise queries and group near-duplicates, so that "sled push without a
   sled" and "hyrox sled push no sled" are one topic. Exact string matching, as
   in the proposal, makes duplicate pages.
3. For each group:
   - **Refresh task:** most impressions land on a page of yours that is relevant
     but ranks poorly. The suggested change is the title, the description or a
     missing section of *that* page.
   - **New guide:** no relevant page gets the impressions, or only the homepage
     does.
4. Score each group by impressions × position opportunity × business fit (does a
   tool or product match?). Set thresholds for a small site: Search Console
   hides rare queries anyway, and 15 impressions in 28 days, the proposal's
   threshold, is noise.
5. While data is thin, fall back to the reconciled seed list (section 9).
6. Show the top five in `/admin/guides/topics`. You choose two or three, or
   reject one with a reason.

There is no Google Trends input. Later, real athlete questions are a better
signal than Trends: anonymised themes from Edge Coach in the app, Talk-to-us
messages, and the plan finder's "unmatched demand" review. That needs a privacy
check in both projects first.

### 4.5 Drafting pipeline

Each stage is saved and resumable. One stage runs per maintenance tick.

1. **Brief** (code, then the model). It sets:
   - the search intent and the angle;
   - which tool to embed and which CTA variant to use;
   - up to six internal links, chosen *from the topic map*, so they are always
     real URLs;
   - the facts to use, and an outline.

   You can add a **coach's note**: a first-hand point only you would make. It is
   the most valuable sentence in the guide, and the part no competitor's engine
   can copy.
2. **Draft** (Gemini, structured output against the block schema, low
   temperature). The model is named in one constant, as in `monthly-review.ts`.
3. **Checks** (code, deterministic). Any failure blocks the draft from review
   until it is fixed:
   - the draft matches the schema, and the slug is derived in code and unique;
   - the first sentence answers the query with a concrete number;
   - every workout block has a beginner option and a race-pace option, each with
     RPE and rest;
   - every load, distance or rep count next to a station name matches the facts
     registry for the stated division;
   - links point only to known routes or allowlisted domains;
   - no banned phrases, and no US spellings from a word list;
   - the word count is within a set band;
   - it is not too similar to any existing guide (shingle overlap);
   - no product claim falls outside the product facts;
   - no medical claims from a blocked list.
4. **Model review.** A second model, ideally from another family (Claude, through
   the SDK the monthly review already uses), scores the draft against the bible
   and lists problems with quotes. It does not rewrite.
5. **Your review** in `/admin/guides/[slug]`:
   - a rendered preview, with check results and review notes beside it;
   - edit any block;
   - approve, or reject with a reason.

   Your edits and reasons are logged, and reviewing them monthly is how the
   bible improves.
6. **Publish.** Set the status, revalidate, and add the guide to the sitemap.
   Then queue URL inspection checks at +3, +7, +14 and +30 days, and propose
   contextual links from two or three older pages (section 4.8).

**Switch:** `CONTENT_ENGINE_MODE` = `off` or `draft`. Nothing publishes without a
person: there is deliberately no auto-publish mode. It ships as `off`.

### 4.6 Guide format: typed blocks, not MDX

| Block | Fields |
|---|---|
| `heading` | level (2 or 3), text |
| `paragraph` | inline text with bold, italic, and links to allowlisted internal routes or external domains |
| `list` | ordered or not, items |
| `callout` | kind (`tip`, `warning`, `coach-note`), text |
| `workout` | title, goal, `beginner {…}`, `racePace {…}`, rest, RPE, notes. Both options are required by the schema, so the bible's scaling rule is enforced by code |
| `table` | header, rows (plain text) |
| `calculator` | `id` (enum: an existing tool id or a new one), preset inputs |
| `cta` | variant id from the CTA registry (section 6.2) |
| `faq` | question and answer pairs, rendered visibly |
| `sources` | title, publisher, optional URL. Only references checked to exist, the same rule as `/una/nerdy-bits` |

Blocks are rendered by the site's own components, with no
`dangerouslySetInnerHTML`.

### 4.7 Pages and SEO plumbing

- **Routes:** `/guides` (a hub, grouped by pillar) and `/guides/[slug]`. Use
  `createMetadata({ type: 'article' })` from `src/lib/seo.ts` and a canonical
  URL.
- **Structured data:** JSON-LD `Article` (author as a `Person`,
  `datePublished`, `dateModified`, publisher) and `BreadcrumbList`. FAQs are
  shown on the page; FAQ JSON-LD is optional and expected to earn no snippet.
- **Author box:** a real name and real experience, plus "Reviewed by … on …".
  This is the most direct quality signal available.
- **Images:** a per-guide OG image from `next/og`, kept out of `public/` (commit
  `b982d11` is why).
- **`sitemap.ts`:** add published guides with their real `lastModified`. The
  route is already dynamic, so it can read Firestore. Teach `sitemap.test.ts`
  about the dynamic route. While there, stop stamping every static page
  `lastModified: new Date()`: Google ignores a `lastmod` that always says "now".
- **Navigation:** add the guides hub to `public/llms.txt` and a "Guides" link to
  the header and footer.
- **Retiring a guide:** set `retired`. With a `redirectTo`, the guide route
  issues a permanent redirect; without one, it returns 410. This is handled in
  the route, not in middleware.

### 4.8 Internal linking

- **New guide to existing pages:** chosen from the topic map in the brief.
- **Existing pages to the new guide:** a `RelatedGuides` component chooses links
  by pillar and tags when the page renders, on guides and on the pillar pages
  (`/how-to-train-for-hyrox`, `/hyrox-training-plan`, `/hybrid-training-program`
  and `/calculators`). No published file is rewritten, unlike the proposal's
  `linker.py`.
- **Contextual links inside older guides:** the engine proposes a sentence and a
  link for two or three of them. You approve each one, and it becomes a new
  revision.

### 4.9 Search Console, without `gum`

- **Property:** `sc-domain:hybridx.club`. Confirm it exists and is
  DNS-verified.
- **API:** enable the Search Console API in the `hybridx-hub` Google Cloud
  project.
- **Identity:**
  - Preferred, with no key at all: add the App Hosting backend's runtime service
    account to Search Console as a **Restricted** user and call the API with
    Application Default Credentials. Before relying on this, check that the
    runtime can mint a token with the `webmasters.readonly` scope.
  - Fallback: a dedicated service account (not the existing Sheets one) with its
    key in a secret. Follow the two-step create-and-grant procedure in CLAUDE.md
    *before* the `apphosting.yaml` entry lands.
- **Access level:** Restricted is enough for search analytics and URL
  inspection. Submit the sitemap once, by hand, in the Search Console UI.
- **Library:** `@googleapis/searchconsole` (smaller than all of `googleapis`).
- **Quotas:** URL Inspection allows 2,000 calls a day and 600 a minute per
  property. This plan uses a few dozen a week.
- **Limit:** URL Inspection *reports* index status. It does not request
  indexing.

### 4.10 Scheduling and operations

- **Scheduling:** run as a step of the existing hourly
  `/api/cron/marketing-maintenance` job, the same way `monthly-review.ts` runs. No
  new scheduler and no new credential: the step is maintenance-class work under
  `CRON_SECRET`. One stage per tick, Search Console harvest weekly, metrics daily.
- **Timeouts:** each stage must finish well inside the platform's request
  timeout. Check the current App Hosting limit before relying on it; staging the
  pipeline exists partly so that no single call has to be long.
- **Cost:** pennies per draft at this cadence. Set a budget alert on the Gemini
  key's billing account anyway, and cap tokens per stage.
- **Status:** `/admin/guides` shows the last run, its stage and any error.
  Failures never publish anything.

## 5. The three calculators

Rules common to all three:

- The arithmetic lives in `src/lib/`, pinned by tests to worked examples you
  write (the `una-maths` and `glasgow-pacing` pattern).
- Styling uses the `hx` tokens, with a contrast check like `magnets.test.ts`.
- Defaults are server-rendered, with no layout shift.
- Each tool gets its own indexable page under `/calculators/…`, an entry in the
  sitemap and the calculators hub, and can be embedded in a guide with presets.

### 5.1 Week planner ("SplitBuilder"). Build first

It conflicts with nothing, its value is obvious, and it is fully deterministic.

- **Inputs:**
  - training days (3, 4 or 5; consider 2 and 6);
  - goal (first HYROX, faster HYROX, ATHX, or 10k plus strength);
  - kit (full gym, basic gym, home, no sled);
  - preferred long-run day.
- **Model:** a rule engine over a session library:
  - sessions: heavy lower, upper, threshold, intervals, easy or zone 2,
    compromised brick, station skill, rest;
  - one template per goal and day count;
  - constraints from the bible: minimum gaps between heavy lower-body work and
    hard running, strength before running when both share a day, no stacked hard
    days in a 3-day week, and kit substitutions.
- **Tests:** the input space is small (a few hundred combinations), so test
  *every* combination against every constraint.
- **Output:**
  - a 7-day grid;
  - "Add to calendar" (`.ics`, through `src/lib/ical-generator.ts`);
  - "Email me this week and the next three", a magnet with immediate delivery
    and the inputs in `extra`.
- **Honest CTA:** "HybridX has 3-, 4- and 5-day programmes built on these rules,
  progressed over 12 weeks." That needs a table mapping goal and day count to an
  app programme id, checked against the app's library. "This exact schedule" is
  not true today.
- **Decision:** whether this becomes the plan finder's result screen rather than
  a separate tool. They ask similar questions.

### 5.2 Strength and engine test ("StrengthRatio")

- **No validated "engine-limited versus force-limited" model exists.** Present
  this as a coach's heuristic ("where to spend your next eight weeks"), not a
  diagnosis.
- **Inputs:**
  - **bodyweight** (the proposal leaves it out, and strength ratios mean nothing
    without it);
  - division;
  - squat, deadlift and press, as an estimated 1RM or reps × weight (reuse the
    one-rep-max calculator's formula);
  - a recent 5k time (reuse the VDOT logic).
- **Output:** relative strength against your thresholds, an aerobic band, and
  one of three verdicts, each with an eight-week focus.
- **Thresholds are yours to set.** They are a coaching judgement, and tests pin
  your worked examples.
- **Safety:** never ask a beginner to test a true 1RM. Accept a 5RM or an
  estimate.

### 5.3 HYROX pacer

- **Already built:**
  - division profiles and the run-fade model in
    `src/lib/shop/glasgow-2027-pacing.ts`, with tests pinned to the printed
    Pacing Pack;
  - the lap structure in `/race`;
  - the split time calculator.
- **Missing:**
  - **a station-time model** for each division and finish time. Its source is a
    decision: the Pacing Pack's tables, your own model, or analysis of public
    results (check the results site's terms before collecting anything);
  - **a rule for "strongest and weakest station"** that moves time budget
    between stations.
- **The Pacing Pack conflict.** The options are:
  - (a) wait until sales close on 14 March 2027;
  - (b) **recommended:** the free tool shows run splits, the Roxzone budget and
    the total station budget. The station-by-station table is emailed (magnet)
    or comes in the pack while it is on sale;
  - (c) make it all free and accept the overlap.
- **Refactor:** move the model out of `src/lib/shop/` into a shared
  `src/lib/hyrox-pacing.ts`, keeping the Glasgow tests green.
- **CTA:** "load this target" needs app work (section 6.3). Until that exists,
  the CTA is about training for the time, not loading it.

## 6. Conversion path and the apps

### 6.1 The ladder

1. A guide answers the question.
2. A tool gives a personal result.
3. **"Email me my result / plan"** (a magnet). The lead's `placement` is the
   guide or tool slug.
4. A nurture journey built in `/admin/marketing/studio`. Per CLAUDE.md, a new
   slug needs nothing in the app repo.
5. Web signup, with the trial.
6. After signup, "get the app".

Why email before signup:

- it is a small step;
- it is recorded on the server, so it counts every conversion without needing
  analytics consent;
- it works today, before the apps are published.

### 6.2 `ContextualCTA`

- **A registry, `src/lib/guides/ctas.ts`.** Each variant has a headline, body,
  destination and tracking id. The model proposes a variant per guide and you
  approve it, so copy is reused and tested rather than invented each day.
- **Copy may only state product facts.**
- **One switch** controls whether store badges appear, for when the apps are
  live.

### 6.3 Handing a result to the app (app repo work)

- **Same device:** link to `app.hybridx.club/signup?seed=<signed token>`. The
  token carries the tool, its inputs and the result, HMAC-signed like
  `lead-tokens.ts` with a new secret shared by the two projects (distinct from
  the existing secrets).
- **Different device:** the inputs ride in the lead's `extra` through the
  bridge. On signup, the app looks up the lead by email and applies the seed.
- **Also needed in hybridx-app:**
  - read and verify the seed, and map it to a programme;
  - **record the signup source** (UTM, seed, referrer) on the user document.
    Today nothing is recorded, so no trial can be credited to a guide;
  - if you want "load this target" to be true, store a target finish time on
    the profile and use it to pace sessions.

### 6.4 When the apps are published

- **Universal links and app links:**
  - serve `apple-app-site-association` and `assetlinks.json` from
    **app.hybridx.club**;
  - add the Associated Domains entitlement on iOS, and intent filters with
    `autoVerify` on Android;
  - add an `appUrlOpen` handler (`@capacitor/app`) that routes the WebView;
  - keep hybridx.club links on the web.
- **No deferred deep linking service.** Firebase Dynamic Links shut down in
  August 2025, and none is needed if signup happens on the web first.
- **Store badges** belong in the welcome email, on the signup success page, and
  as a secondary link on `/app`. Never make them a guide's main CTA while the
  apps cannot sell.
- **Attribution:** App Store Connect campaign links (`pt` and `ct`) and Google
  Play referrer UTMs, one per placement. Aggregate only, but free.
- **If Apple requires in-app purchase** (the app repo already expects this may
  happen): store billing granting the same entitlement Stripe grants (for
  example RevenueCat). Then decide on store pricing, because the stores take a
  commission, and only then CTA straight to the stores.

**Before launch:** export three months of Search Console data for the existing
pages, as the baseline.

| When | Measure | Healthy | Stop and reassess |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 days after each guide | Indexed? | ≥ 80% of guides indexed | < 50% indexed, or "Crawled – currently not indexed" rising |

| Continuous | CTA copy changes | Judged after ≥ 300 visits per variant | — |

Traffic is small, so resist reading noise. `insights.ts` already refuses to
comment below 100 sessions, and the same spirit applies here.

## 8. The coaching bible, rewritten

The proposal's version is a good start but too thin to govern a model. It should
have five parts.

1. **Voice and editorial.**
   - Who HybridX is for.
   - Tone, taken from the app's brand briefing: specific, intelligent and
     performance-focused, avoiding gym-bro, corporate and vague-wellness
     language.
   - UK English, with an explicit word list (programme, kilometre, metre,
     organise, colour, tyre…).
   - Banned openers and phrases.
   - Answer first, with a number.
   - Length bands.
   - **No invented first-person experience.** First-hand material comes only
     from your coach's note.
2. **Training rules.**
   - The interference rules, with *your* numbers for each pairing, not
     "6–24 hours".
   - Strength first when combined with running.
   - Every workout scaled for a beginner and for race pace, with RPE and rest.
   - Progression limits.
   - Safety lines: no max testing for beginners, and "see a professional" for
     pain.
   - What is never prescribed.
3. **Facts.** None in prose. They are injected from the registry for each guide.
4. **Sourcing.** Cite only references checked to exist. Cite books without
   links. Never invent a study. No "research shows" without a citation.
5. **Claims.** Product facts only, plus the "Not claimed" list.

**Agree with the app.** The method should match the app's programmes, so a guide
never contradicts what a subscriber's plan says. Draw the principles from the
app's programme documents (`PROGRAM_COACHING_REVIEW.md` and its companions) and
confirm them yourself.

## 13. Errors in the proposal's code, for whoever builds this

These matter only if any of it is reused.

- `response_mime_type="application/schema+json"`: Gemini structured output
  expects `"application/json"` alongside `response_schema`.
- `model="gemini-2.5-pro"` is hard-coded. Pin whatever is current when it is
  built, in one constant.
- `recommended_calculator` is hard-coded to `"SplitBuilder"` for every Search
  Console topic.
- Deduplication is by exact lowercase string, so near-identical queries become
  separate pages.
- Duplicates are checked only against the engine's own guides, not your
  existing pages, and the `page` dimension is never requested.
- The 15-impression threshold and impression-only sorting ignore position and
  click-through opportunity.
- The whole `sitemap-index.json` is pasted into every prompt. It grows without
  limit; pass the ten most relevant pages instead.
- The model chooses the slug. Derive it in code and check it is unique.
- Every error is caught and `print`ed. A silent fallback hides an expired
  credential for weeks.
- `publish.py` resubmits the sitemap daily (unnecessary) and expects URL
  inspection to drive indexing (it does not).
- `linker.py` appends to published files, which churns pages and conflicts in
  git. Choose related links at render time instead.
- The "Master Build Tracker" (section 7 of the proposal) is empty in the .docx.
  The interactive checklist did not survive export.
